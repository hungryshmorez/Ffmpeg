# Browser app workflows → FFWORKS parity

Generated by `scripts/workflows/parity.py` from the browser app's catalogue (`workflows.js`, `_v3`, `_v4`, `_v5`; `scripts/workflows/dump.js` → `browser_workflows.json`): **227 unique workflows** in 16 categories (the README's "215" is an older count).

## Status, honestly

* **Ported and tested: 86**. *Audio (44):* the *audio-mastering* chains and the tone/effect/pitch/speed/channel workflows of *audio* and *audio-repair-utility*, as audio filter chains (and a clip speed where the browser app changes tempo) applied to an audio clip from the Effects panel (`workflows.rs`, `AddAudioChain`; `tests/audioworkflows.rs`; GUI `workflows.sh`), built exactly as the browser app's `buildAudioFilterChain()` builds them (`scripts/workflows/build_audio.py`). *Video (42):* the colour-grading, retro, stylize and glitch looks whose settings only use sections 5/7/8/9/13/16/19/31 (plus the two flips), as video filter chains on a video clip (`AddVideoChain`; `tests/videoworkflows.rs`; GUI `videowf.sh`), built as `buildVideoFilterChain()` builds them (`scripts/workflows/build_video.py`). Whether they look/sound the same as in the browser app was not compared by eye or ear.
* **Not ported: 141**. Of those, 6 are export/format operations rather than clip workflows and 12 are done natively by existing FFWORKS controls (both noted below). For the rest, FFWORKS has many of the *capabilities* (effects, LUTs, speed/reverse/freeze, transitions, export presets, pixel sort, the mosh/frame/corruption labs) but they are not packaged as one-click workflows and nobody has checked workflow by workflow. Most of the catalogue is settings of the browser app's control panel; what each does lives in `app.js`, so a faithful port means reading that mapping per category (audio is done).
